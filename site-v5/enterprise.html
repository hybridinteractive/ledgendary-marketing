Private by construction, not by promise.

What you don't keep can't leak. Tax ids keep their kind and last four. Signatures keep a keyed fingerprint of the device. Calendar capture keeps titles and durations. And a person's data-subject request anonymises them, while the financial records the law says to keep stay kept.

  • A full tax id is never stored, and there's no column for one.

    Receipt: a vendor's tax id row.

    True story. "EIN · ends 4821" is the whole record. The rest of the number was never written anywhere.

  • Calendar capture keeps a title and a duration. Nothing else gets in.

    Receipt: the calendar row.

    Attendees, descriptions and meeting links are dropped before anything is written, so there's nothing to clean up later.

  • A data-subject request anonymises the person and keeps the books.

    Receipt: a former client contact's record.

    The contact's name and email are gone since Nov 3. The invoices they received are still in the books, because tax law says they stay.

Books you keep versus books you can prove.

Books you keep

  1. A wrong entry gets edited, and the old figure is gone.
  2. "Trust us, nobody changed it" is the audit trail.
  3. A closed month quietly moves when someone fixes a typo.
  4. A report adds dollars to euros and calls it revenue.
  5. A retried notice books the same payment twice.

Books you can prove

  1. Posted entries are reversed, never edited.
  2. A chain anchored by a timestamp authority that isn't us.
  3. Closed periods refuse the change.
  4. Each currency keeps its own total.
  5. One payment, one posting, however many notices.
