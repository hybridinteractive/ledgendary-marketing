True story. Signed by someone who isn't us.

Every posting is hash-chained in the same transaction; if the chain can't record it, the posting doesn't happen. Then the chain is anchored outside the app: the organisation's entries are reduced to one root, a third-party timestamp authority signs it, and the evidence goes into storage nobody can delete before its retention date. Us included.

Closed means closed.

Close October and nothing dated in it can change. An edit to a document in a closed period is refused, not quietly restated somewhere else. And one counter numbers every document inside the transaction that creates it, so a failure takes its number back with it.

Money lands once.

A payment provider sometimes says the same thing twice. The second notice finds the payment already applied and changes nothing. Refunds go the safe way round: charged at the provider first, recorded second. And anything a person can do that takes money off the books asks them who they are first.
